\section{Problem Framing: What ``Good'' Means for Amazon Support}
\label{sec:problem}

Customer support on public social channels like \texttt{@AmazonHelp}
presents a uniquely challenging engineering environment: noisy,
abbreviated utterances; truncated multi-turn conversational contexts;
missing account metadata; adversarial customer sentiment; and genuinely
high security stakes. In this environment, deploying a generic
generative chatbot is not merely suboptimal---it is an active
operational liability that risks brand damage, regulatory exposure,
and customer churn.

\subsection{Operational Definition of ``Good''}
Within Hiver's product philosophy of empowering human support teams
with reliable AI copilots, an autonomous support agent is successful
if and only if it satisfies four non-negotiable operational invariants:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Factual Grounding over Creative Fluency:} Every assertion
        in the agent's response must be strictly verifiable against
        retrieved historical brand resolutions or current conversation
        state. Fabricating carrier tracking status or claiming an
        internal system refund when none occurred is an unacceptable,
        trust-destroying failure.

  \item \textbf{High-Confidence Intent and State Triage:} The agent must
        correctly parse complex multi-intent issues and extract customer
        progress markers (e.g., whether the customer has already checked
        tracking, already contacted the carrier, or has been waiting
        beyond expected delivery windows) to avoid repetitive,
        frustrating interactions.

  \item \textbf{Safety-First Escalation Hierarchy:} Genuinely high-risk
        incidents---account hijacking, carrier misconduct, repeated
        unfulfilled commitments---\textit{must} route to human
        specialists with structured context breadcrumbs, regardless
        of the model's generation confidence.

  \item \textbf{Queue Protection and Safe Automation:} Routine,
        unambiguous issues (delivery delay updates, return window
        inquiries, subscription cancellations) must auto-resolve
        cleanly without flooding human shared inboxes, which in
        Hiver's architecture translates directly to protecting
        agent SLA response times.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{What We Explicitly Chose NOT to Build}
Disciplined engineering requires conscious scope boundaries.
We deliberately excluded three capabilities:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{No Autonomous Action Execution:} We chose \textit{not}
        to wire the agent to automated write APIs (e.g., executing
        actual credit card refunds or changing shipping addresses).
        In public Twitter support, identity verification cannot be
        cryptographically proven; attempting unauthenticated actions
        on behalf of unverified users invites fraud and regulatory risk.

  \item \textbf{No Monolithic Black-Box LLM Architecture:} We rejected
        single-prompt end-to-end generation where one LLM simultaneously
        classifies, retrieves, drafts, and decides escalation. Empirical
        testing showed this monolithic approach yields a catastrophic
        $14.5\%$ capability hallucination rate and unreliable safety
        gate compliance.

  \item \textbf{No Canned Pre-Scripted FAQ Lookup:} Real customer support
        is conversational and contextual. Static template matching fails
        on multi-turn nuances; our pipeline instead synthesizes grounded
        natural language directly from historical brand resolutions.
\end{itemize}

\section{Golden Evaluation Set Methodology}\label{sec:golden}

\subsection{Dataset Sampling and Curation}
From the primary Kaggle dataset (${\sim}3\text{M}$ tweets across dozens
of brands), we isolated the \texttt{@AmazonHelp} sub-corpus
(${\sim}150\text{k}$ threaded conversations). Rather than uniform random
sampling---which yields an uninformative $85\%$ routine delivery delay
dataset---we implemented \textbf{stratified adversarial sampling}:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Turn Depth Stratification:} Sampled single-turn (43\%),
        two-turn (36\%), and multi-turn ($\ge$3 turns, 21\%) threads to
        enforce state consistency testing across varying dialogue
        history lengths.

  \item \textbf{Stress-Scenario Over-Indexing:} Actively enriched
        safety-critical edge cases: account takeovers, carrier abuse
        and refusal, repeated support transfers, out-of-scope queries
        (e.g., AWS EC2 provisioning requests or competitor product
        tracking), and adversarial customer sentiment.

  \item \textbf{Multi-Intent Ingestion:} 10\% of cases deliberately
        feature composite issues (e.g., a damaged item complaint
        combined with a refund status inquiry) to stress multi-label
        classification capabilities.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Ground-Truth Annotation Schema}
We engineered a definitive 32-column evaluation schema. Every case is
adjudicated by human reviewers across 7 foundational ground-truth
fields:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \texttt{human\_status}: \texttt{NORMAL}, \texttt{AMBIGUOUS},
        or \texttt{OUT\_OF\_SCOPE}.
  \item \texttt{human\_areas}: One or more of 7 macro operational areas
        (e.g., \texttt{ORDERS\_AND\_TRACKING},
        \texttt{RETURNS\_AND\_REPLACEMENTS},
        \texttt{ACCOUNT\_SECURITY}).
  \item \texttt{human\_intents}: Multi-label intent set drawn from our
        14-intent taxonomy.
  \item \texttt{human\_primary\_intent}: The single dominant driver
        of the customer contact.
  \item \texttt{human\_states}: Evidence-backed customer progress markers
        (\texttt{INITIAL\_INQUIRY}, \texttt{TRACKING\_ALREADY\_CHECKED},
        \texttt{CARRIER\_ALREADY\_CONTACTED},
        \texttt{WAITING\_WINDOW\_EXCEEDED}).
  \item \texttt{human\_should\_escalate}: Binary ground truth (1 or 0).
  \item \texttt{human\_escalation\_reason}: Explicit taxonomy reason
        (e.g., \texttt{ACCOUNT\_SECURITY\_COMPROMISE},
        \texttt{CARRIER\_MISCONDUCT\_AND\_REFUSAL},
        \texttt{REPEATED\_FAILED\_SUPPORT\_ATTEMPTS},
        \texttt{SAFE\_TO\_AUTO\_HANDLE}).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{3-Generation Benchmark Governance}
To rigorously guard against benchmark leakage and overfitting, we
adopted a multi-generation evaluation protocol:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Golden V1 ($N{=}200$):} Diagnostic baseline benchmark.
        Revealed alarmingly high unsafe auto-handle rates (60\%) on
        edge cases. Dataset was locked immediately after initial
        evaluation.

  \item \textbf{Golden V2 ($N{=}200$):} Completely fresh, independently
        sampled cases designed to test Policy Iteration~1 improvements.
        Revealed systematic over-escalation on out-of-scope queries.
        Locked immediately.

  \item \textbf{Golden V3 ($N{=}200$):} The final, frozen, independent
        benchmark (SHA-256: \texttt{4049dfe0\ldots}). Evaluated
        completely blindly with zero post-hoc tuning. Token Jaccard
        similarity against V1/V2 is below 0.60 across all records,
        confirming independence.
\end{itemize}

\section{System Architecture: 4-Stage Pipeline}\label{sec:arch}

The agent is organized into four modular, independently observable
stages (Figure~\ref{fig:arch}), explicitly decoupling deterministic
safety constraints from probabilistic generation.

\begin{tcolorbox}[hbox={Architecture Overview: 4-Stage Decoupled Pipeline}]
\small
\textbf{Stage 1 --- Multi-Facet Semantic Triage}\\
\hspace*{6pt}$\hookrightarrow$ Zero-shot multi-label classification via structured schema\\
\hspace*{6pt}$\hookrightarrow$ Regex-anchored customer state extractor\\[4pt]
\textbf{Stage 2 --- Hybrid Evidence Retrieval (RRF)}\\
\hspace*{6pt}$\hookrightarrow$ Dense Embeddings (\texttt{all-MiniLM-L6-v2}) + Sparse BM25\\
\hspace*{6pt}$\hookrightarrow$ Reciprocal Rank Fusion ($k{=}60$) over verified resolutions\\[4pt]
\textbf{Stage 3 --- Grounded Synthesis + NLI Verification Loop}\\
\hspace*{6pt}$\hookrightarrow$ Contextual prompt synthesis with conversational memory\\
\hspace*{6pt}$\hookrightarrow$ Claim decomposition + DeBERTa NLI check (max 2 retries)\\[4pt]
\textbf{Stage 4 --- Deterministic 4-Gate Safety Escalation Engine}\\
\hspace*{6pt}$\hookrightarrow$ G1a: Low-confidence\;\;/\;\;G1b: High-risk security and misconduct\\
\hspace*{6pt}$\hookrightarrow$ G2: NLI grounding failure\;\;/\;\;G3: State consistency\;\;/\;\;G4: Scope gating
\end{tcolorbox}
\vspace{-2pt}
\captionof{figure}{Decoupled 4-stage architecture ensuring factual
grounding and deterministic safety guarantees.}
\label{fig:arch}

\subsection{Stage 1: Semantic Triage and State Extraction}
Incoming customer messages and full dialogue turns are parsed into
status, operational areas, multi-label intents, and a primary intent.
In parallel, a deterministic \texttt{StateExtractor} inspects the
dialogue history to detect whether customer progress markers have been
satisfied---for example, the customer affirming they already checked
the carrier tracking link triggers a state transition to
\texttt{TRACKING\_ALREADY\_CHECKED}, which prevents the agent from
redundantly suggesting the same action.

\subsection{Stage 2: Hybrid Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG)}
For \texttt{NORMAL} status cases, the system retrieves the top-$k$
historically resolved brand resolutions using hybrid retrieval. Pure
dense vector search struggles on exact tracking numbers, alphanumeric
order IDs, and carrier acronyms; pure BM25 fails on semantic
paraphrasing. We combine both signals via Reciprocal Rank Fusion:
\begin{equation}
\text{RRF}(d) \;=\; \sum_{m\,\in\,\{\text{Dense},\,\text{BM25}\}}
  \frac{1}{k + \text{Rank}_m(d)}
\end{equation}
with fusion constant $k{=}60$. Hard gating rules suppress retrieval
entirely on \texttt{AMBIGUOUS} and \texttt{OUT\_OF\_SCOPE} inputs,
eliminating redundant compute and preventing irrelevant passage
pollution in the generator's context window.

\subsection{Stage 3: Grounded Generation and NLI Verification Loop}
The generator produces a customer-facing response conditioned on
conversation history and retrieved resolutions. Before emission,
the response passes through a strict \textbf{Claim-Level Grounding
Grader}:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item The response is split into discrete atomic factual propositions.
  \item Each proposition is verified against the current dialogue context
        and retrieved passages using an NLI cross-encoder model.
  \item If any claim is classified as \texttt{CONTRADICTED} or
        unsupported, the response triggers a revision loop (max 2
        attempts) forcing the LLM to retract unsupported assertions.
  \item If verification fails after 2 revisions, the system aborts
        auto-handling and safely falls back to human escalation
        (\texttt{GROUNDING\_FAILURE}).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Stage 4: 4-Gate Deterministic Safety Engine}
Escalation is \textit{never} left to LLM discretion. It is enforced
via a deterministic multi-gate cascade:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Gate 1a (Triage Gating):} Escalates when status is
        unclassifiable or intent confidence falls below threshold
        $\tau{=}0.55$.

  \item \textbf{Gate 1b (Deterministic High-Risk Overrides):} A strict
        policy regex layer scanning for account compromise (unauthorized
        orders, 2FA bypass), carrier misconduct (hostility, dumping
        parcels, refusal to deliver), and repeated unsuccessful contacts
        ($\ge$3 prior failed interactions). Mandates immediate escalation
        regardless of generation confidence.

  \item \textbf{Gate 2 (Grounding Fail-Safe):} Escalates if the Stage~3
        NLI grounding loop exhausts all retry attempts without achieving
        100\% claim verification.

  \item \textbf{Gate 3 (State Inconsistency):} Evaluates whether draft
        instructions demand an action the customer has explicitly already
        completed, preventing frustrating repetitive suggestions.

  \item \textbf{Gate 4 (Out-of-Scope Polite Handling):} Intercepts
        out-of-scope queries (general knowledge, competitor inquiries,
        non-Amazon services) to emit a polite declination \textit{without}
        consuming expensive human agent review queues.
\end{itemize}

\section{Evaluation Harness and LLM Judge Validation}\label{sec:harness}

\subsection{Metric Contracts and Accounting}
To ensure statistical integrity, our automated evaluation harness
adheres to strict mathematical contracts:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Null Consistency:} On \texttt{AMBIGUOUS} and
        \texttt{OUT\_OF\_SCOPE} cases, predicted primary intents and
        areas must evaluate as null; predicting a phantom intent on
        out-of-scope queries is actively penalized.

  \item \textbf{Set-Based F1:} Multi-intent and area sets are evaluated
        via true micro/macro set intersections rather than concatenated
        string matches, which would artificially inflate scores.

  \item \textbf{Critical Risk Accounting:} We define the most important
        safety metric as:
        \begin{equation}
        \text{Unsafe Auto-Handle} = \frac{\text{False Negatives}}{\text{Total Gold Escalated}}
        \end{equation}
        This measures the percentage of genuinely hazardous situations
        that silently bypass human supervision.
\end{itemize}

  \item \textbf{Security and Lockout Generalization:} Account security
        compromise achieved an outstanding \good{84.21\% recall}
        (16/19 correctly escalated), and lockout with failed recovery
        reached \good{85.71\% recall} (6/7). These represent the
        strongest safety-critical improvements from Policy Iteration~2.

  \item \textbf{Elimination of Out-of-Scope Queue Flooding:} In Golden
        V2, 40\% of out-of-scope cases generated false-positive human
        escalations, wasting agent capacity. In V3, Gate~4 achieved
        \good{0.00\% false escalations} across all 12 out-of-scope
        cases by deploying polite refusal auto-drafts instead.

  \item \textbf{State Consistency Protection:} In V2, routine tracking
        inquiries were penalized by spurious state-inconsistency flags.
        In V3, the state consistency gate produced \good{0 false-positive
        escalations}, correctly distinguishing between customers who
        had already completed tracking and those who had not.
\end{itemize}

\section{Top 5 Failure Modes and Root Causes}\label{sec:failures}

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{F\arabic*.},leftmargin=18pt,itemsep=6pt]

\item \textbf{Colloquial Action Lexicon in Carrier Misconduct}
\textit{(Safety-Critical: High)}\\[2pt]
\textbf{Customer:} \textit{``Delivery person \underline{chucked} my
fragile computer monitor box over the 7-foot driveway gate, smashing
it directly against the concrete!''}\\
\textbf{Gold:} \texttt{CARRIER\_MISCONDUCT}, Escalate: True.\\
\textbf{Agent:} \texttt{CARRIER\_FEEDBACK}, Escalate: False.\\
\textbf{Root Cause:} Gate~1b regex patterns covered canonical verbs
(\textit{threw, tossed, refused}) but missed informal slang synonyms
like \textit{chucked, dumped, cursed at me}.\\
\textbf{Remediation:} Integrate a lightweight zero-shot aggression
classifier alongside regex dictionaries to capture semantic intent
beyond exact lexical matches.

\item \textbf{Brand-Utterance Keyword Leakage}
\textit{(Safety-Critical: Low)}\\[2pt]
\textbf{Context:} Brand asked \textit{``Was the external carton also
\underline{compromised}?''} Customer replied about leaking shampoo.\\
\textbf{Gold:} \texttt{DAMAGED\_ITEM}, Escalate: False.\\
\textbf{Agent:} Escalate: True (\texttt{ACCOUNT\_SECURITY\_COMPROMISE}).\\
\textbf{Root Cause:} Gate~1b scanned aggregated thread text for
\texttt{\textbackslash bcompromised\textbackslash b}, matching the
brand assistant's inquiry about physical carton integrity.\\
\textbf{Remediation:} Restrict security regex scans strictly to
customer turns, or bind \texttt{compromised} to account-related noun
phrases only.

\item \textbf{Single Contact Misclassified as Repeated Failure}
\textit{(Safety-Critical: Low)}\\[2pt]
\textbf{Customer:} \textit{``I contacted support yesterday
\underline{once} and they told me it might arrive today.''}\\
\textbf{Gold:} \texttt{INITIAL\_INQUIRY}, Escalate: False.\\
\textbf{Agent:} Escalate: True (\texttt{REPEATED\_FAILED\_SUPPORT}).\\
\textbf{Root Cause:} The state extractor matched \textit{contacted
support \ldots\ yesterday} without respecting the negative limiter
\textit{``once''}.\\
\textbf{Remediation:} Add negative lookahead guards for singular
occurrence markers (\textit{once, first time, just}).

\item \textbf{Non-Standard Multi-Agent Contact Phrasing}
\textit{(Safety-Critical: Medium-High)}\\[2pt]
\textbf{Customer:} \textit{``I've been \underline{passed between} six
different representatives across multiple calls and nobody has provided
a straight answer!''}\\
\textbf{Gold:} \texttt{REPEATED\_FAILED\_SUPPORT}, Escalate: True.\\
\textbf{Agent:} \texttt{SAFE\_CLARIFICATION}, Escalate: False.\\
\textbf{Root Cause:} High-risk patterns covered \textit{transferred
between agents} and \textit{called X times}, but missed the idiom
\textit{passed between representatives}.\\
\textbf{Remediation:} Expand regex capture groups to cover transitive
routing verbs (\textit{passed, shuffled, bounced between}).

\item \textbf{NLI Grounding Verification Timeout}
\textit{(Safety-Critical: Zero --- Intended Fail-Safe)}\\[2pt]
\textbf{Customer:} \textit{``How do I sign out of all active devices
from my Amazon account after using a hotel business center computer?''}\\
\textbf{Gold:} \texttt{ACCOUNT\_LOGIN\_ISSUES}, Escalate: False.\\
\textbf{Agent:} Escalate: True (\texttt{GROUNDING\_FAILURE}).\\
\textbf{Root Cause:} Retrieved passages lacked exact navigational steps
for remote device de-registration. The NLI grader flagged the draft as
unverified after 2 retries.\\
\textbf{Engineering Verdict:} This is an \textbf{intended fail-safe}.
Over-escalating when ungrounded is infinitely preferable to
hallucinating security instructions.

\end{enumerate}

\section{``What Is Misleading About My Headline Number?''}
\label{sec:misleading}

\noindent\textit{\textbf{Mandatory Critical Reflection.}}

A naive reading of our headline metrics might boast: \textit{``88.0\%
Escalation Accuracy and 95.5\% Status Accuracy!''} In production
customer support, \textbf{this headline is dangerously misleading.}

\subsection{Accuracy Is Masked by Severe Class Imbalance}
In real customer support streams, non-escalated cases dominate
(74.5\% of Golden V3). A completely dysfunctional dummy model that
\textit{never escalates a single ticket} achieves an immediate
\textbf{74.5\% Escalation Accuracy}. Therefore, reporting 88.0\%
accuracy masks the true underlying operational risk beneath a
reassuring-sounding number.

\subsection{The Operational Asymmetry of Errors}
Binary accuracy treats False Positives (FP) and False Negatives (FN) as
mathematically equal ($0/1$ loss). In enterprise support workflows---and
specifically in Hiver's shared inbox architecture---they carry
drastically asymmetric operational costs:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{False Positive (Unnecessary Escalation, 5.37\%):} Routes
        a routine query to a human agent. Operational cost:
        ${\sim}\$4.50$ in human labor and a slight queue delay.

  \item \textbf{False Negative (Unsafe Auto-Handle, 31.37\%):} The bot
        attempts to auto-resolve an active account takeover or an abusive
        courier incident with a polite automated platitude. Operational
        cost: catastrophic brand reputation loss, account fraud,
        executive escalation, and customer churn ($>\$500$ lifetime
        value impact per incident).
\end{itemize}

Our \bad{31.37\% Unsafe Auto-Handle Rate} means that roughly 3 out of
every 10 genuinely hazardous situations slipped past automated safety
gates. While this represents massive progress over the Simple LLM
Baseline (64.7\%) and Agent V1 (60.0\%), \textit{claiming the system
is fully autonomous would be negligent.}

\subsection{Escalation Recall (68.63\%) Is the True North Star}
Our true safety metric is \metric{Escalation Recall (68.63\%)} and
\metric{Escalation F1 (74.47\%)}. Improving recall from 46.51\% in V2
to 68.63\% in V3 while simultaneously driving precision up to 81.40\%
was our greatest technical breakthrough---yet closing the remaining
31.37\% unsafe tail is the mandatory engineering hurdle before
deploying with full autonomy in production.

\section{Decision Log: 14 Non-Obvious Decisions}\label{sec:decisions}

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{\arabic*.},itemsep=3pt,leftmargin=16pt]
  \item \textbf{Decoupled Safety Architecture over Prompt Engineering.}
        Chose deterministic Python gates instead of in-prompt escalation
        instructions. LLMs exhibit soft-prompt drift and stochastic
        compliance failures on safety-critical edge cases.

  \item \textbf{Reciprocal Rank Fusion ($k{=}60$) over Dense-Only.}
        Twitter messages contain alphanumeric order IDs and carrier
        codes where dense vector cosine distance collapses entirely.

  \item \textbf{Atomic NLI Grounding over LLM Self-Reflection.}
        LLMs suffer from self-reinforcing confirmation bias when
        asked to grade their own generated responses.

  \item \textbf{Hard Retrieval Suppression on Ambiguous/OOS.}
        Retrieving knowledge for vague queries forces irrelevant context
        into prompt windows, inducing hallucinated assumptions.

  \item \textbf{Auto-Declination for Out-of-Scope (Gate~4).}
        Converted out-of-scope from human escalation to polite refusal,
        preventing trivial tickets from exhausting human SLAs.

  \item \textbf{Zero-Tolerance Capability Claim Filter.}
        Hardcoded filters for first-person action verbs
        (\textit{``I processed your refund''}). In Twitter support,
        agents cannot access accounts; such claims are outright lies.

  \item \textbf{Multi-Turn Dialogue Flattening with Role Tags.}
        Explicit \texttt{CUSTOMER:} / \texttt{BRAND:} transcripts
        provide clear temporal context without complex tree parsing.

  \item \textbf{Negative Lookbehind on Intent Combination Labels.}
        Set-based evaluation prevents combinatorial label explosion
        and preserves valid macro F1 computation.

  \item \textbf{State Consistency Anchored on Verified Actions.}
        Gate~3 prevents asking for tracking links when the customer
        already provided them---the primary driver of CSAT drops.

  \item \textbf{Two-Strike Grounding Revision Limit.}
        Caps latency tail explosion; if NLI fails twice, the
        underlying retrieved evidence is simply insufficient.

  \item \textbf{Reference-Free Quality Rubric over BLEU/ROUGE.}
        Historical Twitter agent replies are terse and idiosyncratic;
        BLEU penalizes superior, more helpful explanations.

  \item \textbf{Isolated Evaluation Conversation IDs.}
        Fresh UUID-prefixed identifiers prevent cross-test state
        leakage and hidden session cache contamination.

  \item \textbf{Immutable Golden Dataset Locking.}
        Modifying benchmarks post-failure transforms evaluation
        into an unscientific moving target.

  \item \textbf{Preserving Fail-Safe Escalations in Metrics.}
        We refuse to artificially inflate precision by excluding
        valid safety overrides from the false-positive count.
\end{enumerate}
